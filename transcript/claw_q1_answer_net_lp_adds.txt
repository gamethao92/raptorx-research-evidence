1. The Priority Metric: Net LP Adds/Removals

To validate this signal, you should pull Net LP (Liquidity Provider) Adds first.

The Logic: On a DEX pool with only $425k daily volume, the price is dictated by the constant product formula ($x \times y = k$). If a liquidity provider removes a significant portion of the pool, the "depth" thins. In this scenario, even a small buy order creates a disproportionate price spike that triggers a MACD crossover.
The Filter:
Thin-Book Noise: MACD flips bullish, but Net LP is negative. This means liquidity is exiting, making the price volatile and the signal "fake."
Genuine Demand: MACD flips bullish, and Net LP is positive. This suggests "sticky" capital is entering the pool, providing a structural floor for the price move.

Technical analysis (TA) relies on the "Wisdom of the Crowd." When the "crowd" is just a few traders, TA degrades into a random walk.

The Volume Threshold: For a 15-minute timeframe, indicators typically lose predictive power when daily volume drops below $1M – $2M.

A single $2,000 market buy—insignificant in a liquid market—represents nearly 50% of the candle's volume. This creates a "slope change" that the MACD interprets as a "trend," despite having zero institutional or retail follow-through.
